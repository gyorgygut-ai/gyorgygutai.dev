# 5. CV Voice & Personality

Voice and personality guidelines for the CV (site + PDF), distilled from discussion. Overrides cookie-cutter career advice where they conflict.

## The Voice Principle

The "I do…" sections are the differentiator — confident, casual, first-person, human. Keep them. Most portfolios open with "My Skills: React, Node…" and blend into noise. "I do AI" with "agent orchestration, context engineering, agent skill design" is genuinely distinctive.

The casualness isn't decoration — it's the positioning statement itself.

## Kill the Cookie-Cutter Metrics

The "increased X by 22%" filler-metric style is a cargo cult — everyone knows the numbers are invented, so they stopped proving anything. A number that smells like a filler metric reads worse than no number.

Rule:

- **Numbers that fall out of the story naturally** → keep. Real, specific, a byproduct of the work (e.g. BBPro 36x performance, 45% memory reduction).
- **Numbers that exist only to be a number** → kill.
- **Personality lines + real tech** → the rest of the space.

## The DB-Drop Line

> "Kept my client's agent from nuking the production DB — and was completely prepared to recover when it did anyway."

What it communicates: enough real agent systems shipped to know the failure modes; prevention has a ceiling, recovery doesn't; maturity = the undo workflows, not the hope. That's the most senior thing you can say about AI work.

Requirements:

- Recovery workflow needs a tech-backed echo somewhere (backups, rollback, point-in-time restore) — attitude gets the interview, keywords get past the ATS. Two halves of one claim.
- Don't throw the client under the bus — own the story.

## Personality Lines — Identity, Not Humour

These aren't jokes. In today's AI world the terminology changes every three days and nobody knows what anyone is anymore. Labels like "AI Liaison" / "Vibe Checker" are an honest way of saying: I don't know what to call myself, but I'm still me, doing what I can, wearing all the hats required to make the project successful.

- **"AI Liaison"** — positioning, not just personality: "I translate between business needs and AI systems." Survives even a conservative PDF.
- **"Vibe Checker"** — the same identity statement in lighter clothing. Not a punchline.
- **DB-drop line** — evidence of that identity: shipped enough agent systems to know the failure modes, and built the undo workflows.

## Keywords Are Separate

Personality is additive, never a substitute for keywords. Keep `Technologies:` lines intact on every project — that's the ATS evidence layer (Tasks 2–3 built it). Trim narrative bullets for length, don't cut keyword carriers.

## Site vs PDF Voice Split

- **Website:** go hard — personality lines are gold here.
- **PDF:** keep some casualness too — the personality lines belong in the PDF as well.
- **Interview:** start serious, loosen as the mood allows.

## Tonal Match

"Project History" previously read dry next to the "I do…" sections. Fix by layering personality into project bullets so the tone is consistent. Structure plan:

- Fewer bullets per project (to fit PDF)
- Each bullet = personality line OR story-born metric OR tech
- `Technologies:` line stays intact
